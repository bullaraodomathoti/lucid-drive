\title{\lucid{}: A Large-Scale Dataset for Language-grounded Causal Inference and

\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background.}
Large language models (LLMs) are transforming autonomous driving (AD) by enabling
natural-language scene understanding, commonsense reasoning, and explainable
decision-making. However, existing language-annotated driving datasets predominantly
focus on perceptual question-answering and fail to provide the causal and
counterfactual reasoning annotations that are essential for training and evaluating
LLM-based driving agents. This representational gap fundamentally limits progress
in explainable AD.

\noindent\textbf{Methods.}
We introduce \lucid{} (\textbf{L}anguage-grounded \textbf{U}nderstanding for
\textbf{C}ausal \textbf{I}nference and \textbf{D}ecision-making), a large-scale
multimodal driving dataset collected across six global cities on three continents
over 18 months. \lucid{} comprises 12,847 richly annotated driving scenarios
(2,312,460 frames) captured by an eight-camera surround-view system, LiDAR,
radar, and HD maps under seven weather conditions and four lighting states.
The dataset contains 847,293 language annotations organised into five types:
causal chain QA pairs (312,456), counterfactual QA pairs (198,327), planning
instruction pairs (156,832), scene descriptions (89,247), and risk-assessment
annotations (90,431). A three-stage human annotation pipeline with 234 professional
annotators yielded a mean inter-annotator agreement of $\kappa = 0.81$.

\noindent\textbf{Results.}
We define four benchmark tasks---Causal Scene Understanding (CSU), Counterfactual
Reasoning (CFR), Instruction-following Navigation (IFN), and Risk-Aware Planning
(RAP)---and evaluate six state-of-the-art vision-language models. The best-performing
model (GPT-4V) achieves a BLEU-4 of 18.4 on CSU, an accuracy of 58.3\% on CFR, a
success rate of 42.7\% on IFN, and a collision-avoidance rate of 78.3\% on RAP,
revealing substantial room for improvement and confirming that causal and
counterfactual reasoning remains an open challenge.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.}
\lucid{} establishes the first comprehensive benchmark for causal and counterfactual
reasoning in LLM-based autonomous driving. The dataset, annotation tools, evaluation
protocols, and baseline code are publicly released to accelerate research in
explainable and trustworthy autonomous driving systems.
\end{abstract}
